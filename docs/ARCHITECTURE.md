# Architecture

Phase 0 built a thin, real version of every module on the path from a scene to a video. This document says what each module does today and what is still planned. Anything not listed under "built" does not exist yet.

```
script + narration (word timings)                                   [planned, phase 4]
        │
        ▼
scene (TypeScript) ──► look ──► motion ──► physics bake ──► camera ──► render ──► ffmpeg
   │                    │         │             │                         │
   └── string tables    palette   intents,      rigid bodies, springs,    Skia GPU: layers, depth blur,
       per language     + light   springs       particles; cache keyed    glow, atmosphere, vignette
                                                by versions
        ▼
checks (before render): types, palette laws, string tables, text fit per language
review (no render needed): the motion of every node as findings, ghost trails, speed graphs;
                           the soundness of the simulation as findings, close-ups
sound (no render needed):  the events of a shot; its sound track from a bank of sounds, in measure
```

## Modules

| Module | Built (phase 0) | Planned |
|---|---|---|
| `core` | 60 fps time base and the 1/240 s simulation step; seeded random with named sub-streams; easing; second-order dynamics (frequency, damping, response) with presets; seeded 1D and 2D noise; stable hashing of plain data | — |
| `shape` | circle, capsule, rounded convex polygon (regular, rectangle, explicit corners), blob, region (a free-form closed outline through points, concave allowed, with its middle at the largest circle that fits in it); outline, extent and reach of a shape without a drawing library; paths; thin helpers over the path operations (difference, intersection, union, shift, round) | trim and interpolate helpers, generators (L-systems, procedural bodies, scattering); corners with different radii |
| `look` | palette by role (deep, dark, mid, accent, light) and a validator for the measured laws; one light direction per shot; shadow crescent, stacked deep shadow, lit rim, highlight stroke and optional coloured rim light, all derived from the shape; glow as line + halo + background lift; atmospheric perspective as a colour transform (paler, less saturated, toward the atmosphere colour, blended around the colour wheel so that nothing turns gray) | palettes generated from a mood; layered outlines |
| `scene` | shot, depth layers (parallax, blur and atmospheric perspective by depth), nodes: shape, group (optionally upright: it keeps its own rotation on a parent that turns), link, stroke (a line along a free-form path, solid, dashed or dotted, placed like a shape), label, text (free-standing lines from a string key, placed like a shape, with an optional box and no pointer; with a count, a counter whose number is run by `countTo`), eye (with lids), emitter; rigid-body option on shapes; timeline keys anchored to seconds; keys that change the background of the shot over time (its gradient, the colour far layers fade toward, its vignette), with the colours run round the colour wheel and the tint of every layer following the atmosphere of the frame; factories and intents that refuse an option they do not take; compiler that validates the whole shot; pure evaluation of a frame into a display list, in which a faded node and its subtree form a group; a shape as a cover (glow does not shine through it), as a backdrop for labels, or as a content with a level (it fills its outline from the bottom, its surface ripples, its children ride on it); clipping of a node to the outline of a shape; rigid bodies that are not drawn until their release | keys anchored to narration words; rigs; more node types (bubble, title, counter) |
| `motion` | intents `moveTo` (anticipation, arc, overshoot, settle), `popIn`, `popOut`, `turnTo` (to an angle with an overshoot, or with a speed profile and a speed: a spin that starts from rest or comes to rest; or with a speed keyed over time, joined smoothly: a wheel that slows in steps and turns on), `scaleTo` (lasting size, chained, composing with pops and bumps; with two factors and a pivot, a lasting change along the node's own axes), `fadeTo` (opacity), `bump`, `drawOn` (a link; a stroke to a share of its length, key after key, with a speed profile), `solidTo` (the dashes of a stroke close), `bendTo` (the bend of a link changes: it sags and pulls taut), `reshape` (the outline of a region, or the line of a stroke, runs point for point to another, as one shape with one light all the way), `pulse` (a light along a link, or along a stroke between two shares of its length, with a speed profile and a bump of a named node on arrival), `openTo` (eye lids), `blink`; `stagger` over a group; spring-driven secondary motion (`follow`, `sway`) baked per frame; idle wander; speed profiles for a move (settle, ease, steady, in, out); a travel whose speed is keyed over time, joined smoothly; with known moments of leaving and arriving; `hop` (a parabola with an exact take-off and landing); free-form paths measured by their own length and `travel` along them at a known speed, with rolling and facing along the path; `levelTo`; `squash` (a kick that changes the proportions and keeps the area); keys that start before the shot | blending of overlapping moves, more intents, simple rigs under the simple-forms rule |
| `physics` | Rapier 2D wrapper: bodies from shapes, fixed step 1/240 s, style defaults (lower gravity, more drag, low restitution), release of a body at a given time, a prewarm that runs the world before frame 0, contact events with peak impulse; particle system (continuous emission, bursts, bursts on contacts, gravity or wind, drag, turbulence, prewarm; particles that shrink away or keep their size); bake of bodies, springs and particles; on-disk cache | joints, kinematic hand-off, goal forces, seed search; gravity and damping per body, bodies that can be retired, collision filters; XPBD soft bodies and ropes; PBF fluid; boids; particle collisions and sub-emitters |
| `camera` | steady drift plus low-frequency wander, exponential push-in, parallax view per depth layer; instead of the straight drift, a free-form path: round a closed loop or there and back at a set speed, or once over the shot with eased ends; keys of the timeline that zoom it (as a ratio, across orders of magnitude) and move it at their moments, chained, with the drift, the wander and the push-in on top and the keyed zoom reaching a layer as the power of its parallax; closed-form in the frame number | spring follow |
| `text` | per-language string tables; one bundled OFL family (Latin, Latin Extended, Cyrillic, Vietnamese) registered under the engine's own family name; auto-fit layout (wrap at spaces, keep words joined by a no-break space together, balance, shrink, report overflow); label geometry; the block of a free-standing text (aligned left, centre or right, with an optional box); counters (a number written the way the language writes numbers, its digits in cells of one width, laid out for its widest value); subtitles from the word timings of a narration (cues cut at sentences and pauses, written as .srt, .vtt and .ytt) | a check that the font has every glyph of a string; shaping and fallback fonts for all scripts (CJK, Arabic, Devanagari…), right-to-left text, bubbles, titles, counters |

| `audio` | the events of a shot: every intent, every contact and every burst, on its frame, with what is seen of it (size, speed and place on screen, and whether it is seen at all), and for a move how it goes (its fastest moment, its two ends, its way on screen); a bank of sounds that a production brings (sounds by key, a table from kinds of events to sounds, curves of gain against size, speed and impulse, density rules with quiet defaults); the plan of what is heard (one sound per event, only what is seen, thresholds, merging, gaps, a cap on the starts in a window and on the voices at once, priorities, a seeded jitter of pitch and gain; rules that a sound sets for its own events, and showers: runs of a sound that stands apart from the limits of the track; a sound fitted to a move: its loudest point on the fastest moment of the move, its pan with the node, its take by the length of the move); the track of a shot: 48 kHz stereo, each sound on the first sample of its frame, panned by its place on screen, the same bytes for the same scene, bank and seed; PCM WAV read and written without a library; the sound sheet: a tile for every sound, with the frame at which it starts and its node ringed | narration import, music bed with ducking, the mix of a film; sounds that follow a node or last as long as an action |
| `checks` | strict `tsc --noEmit`; unit and determinism tests on `node:test`; palette laws; string tables complete in every language; text fit per language, for labels and free-standing texts (overflow, leaving the frame, overlaps as errors; a label covering a node other than its target as a warning); the findings of the motion review and of the physics review in short form, as warnings; the sound keys of a shot against a bank of sounds, when one is given | — |
| `review` | the motion review, for reviewers that cannot watch a video: the series of every node, link, emitter, spring and of the camera at every frame, in the space of the layer and on screen; findings from it (jump, abrupt start and stop, mechanical move, strobing speed, springs that ring or never bounce, dead areas of the frame) with every threshold in one file; the level of a content judged like a move; what is wholly behind a cover or outside its clip left out; the life of the background in numbers (the camera's drift, travel and the box it stays in, the slide of every layer, the speed of the particles); intended exceptions marked in the scene; a report as capped text and complete JSON; ghost trails and speed graphs drawn on the engine's canvas. | `cli` | `check`, `bake`, `still`, `strip`, `sheet`, `render`, `motion`, `trail`, `graph`, `physics`, `events`, `sfx`; scene modules from any directory; output and cache under the caller's working directory | `preview` window, per-language batch render |

Dependencies point one way: `core` ← `shape` ← `look`; `motion` uses `core`; `scene` uses all of these plus `text` and `camera`; `physics` bakes what `scene` describes; `render` draws what `scene` evaluates; `review` reads what `scene` evaluates and draws its pictures with `render`; `audio` reads the series of `review` and the contacts of the bake, and draws nothing but its sound sheet, with `render`; `checks` and `cli` sit on top.

## Data flow

1. **Describe.** A scene module builds a shot with the factory functions. The result is plain data: no functions, no classes. `shot()` validates it as a whole.
2. **Compile.** The node trees are flattened in draw order, references and colours are resolved, the timeline is turned into per-node tracks, and every body, spring and emitter gets a slot in the bake. The scene hash is computed here, over everything except the string tables.
3. **Bake.** One process simulates everything with history at the fixed step and stores it per frame: rigid bodies (and their contact events), then springs, then particles. The pose stored for frame *n* is the state at *n*/60 s.
4. **Evaluate.** `evaluate(shot, bake, frame, language)` returns a display list: world transforms, link curves, pulse positions, label layouts, particle slices, opacities, the camera and the view of each layer. A node that is not fully opaque becomes a group holding its subtree; a node clipped to a shape, and a node that is being squashed, are wrapped in the same way. It has no state.
5. **Draw.** The renderer composites the display list on Skia and reads back raw RGBA. A group is drawn into a picture of its own and composited at its opacity. What feeds the halo of a layer is drawn in the same order as the layer itself, so a cover can erase the glow drawn before it. Atmospheric perspective is applied to colours, not to pixels: every colour a far layer draws with is transformed before it is used.
6. **Encode.** For a video, worker processes each take a contiguous frame range, pipe their frames into their own ffmpeg encoder, and the chunks are concatenated without re-encoding. Workers report their progress to the parent; if one fails, the parent stops the rest, removes the partial files and fails with that worker's frame range.

Because frames are pure, a still, a motion strip or a contact sheet never needs a rendered video.

## The motion review

A reviewer that cannot watch a video still has to judge motion. The review answers from the scene data, in three steps:

1. **Sample.** `sampleMotion` evaluates the shot at every frame without drawing: world transforms, opacities, the camera and the view of every layer. It keeps, for every placed node, its position, rotation, scale and opacity in the space of its layer, and its own motion in the space of its parent; for links, emitters and springs what their checks need. A node that nothing animates is sampled once.
2. **Detect.** Detectors read the series and return findings: plain data with a kind, a subject, a frame range, the measured value, the threshold and a usual fix. Every threshold is in `src/review/thresholds.ts`. Findings that the scene marks with `intended()` are taken out and listed as honoured.
3. **Show.** The report is printed as capped text and written as complete JSON. A ghost trail draws chosen nodes at every k-th frame over a dimmed frame; a speed graph plots speed and whatever else changes over time, with the findings marked.

The same scene always gives the same report and the same pictures. Guide: [MOTION-REVIEW.md](MOTION-REVIEW.md).

## The physics review

Whether the simulation is sound is answered from the bake, in one pass over the frames:

1. **Place.** Every rigid body gets the collider the world gave it: a convex core (a point, a segment, a polygon) and a radius around it, at the baked pose of the frame. The review builds this geometry itself and does not ask the physics library; a test holds the two against each other.
2. **Pair.** Bodies whose bounding circles are near are found by a sweep along x; for each pair the distance, or the depth, of the two colliders is worked out. From it come the overlaps, the piles (bodies that touch) and what a body can have touched within a frame.
3. **Detect.** Detectors read the poses and the pairs: an overlap that lasts, a centre that crosses a fixed body or a gap, a body below everything, swings of a body at rest, a pile that travels at the end, speed that nothing in touch can have given, a path bent by a body that is not drawn, numbers that are not numbers. Every threshold is in `src/review/physics-thresholds.ts`. What is not seen is not reported.
4. **Show.** The report has the form of the motion report. A close-up draws a few frames of the place, enlarged, with the bodies ringed.

It is synchronous and takes a fraction of a second on a shot with five hundred bodies, so it runs in every `skink3 check`. When the physics of the engine changes, this review changes with it in the same piece of work. Guide: [PHYSICS-REVIEW.md](PHYSICS-REVIEW.md).

## The sound of a shot

The sound effects of a shot are made from what happens in it, in three steps, none of which draws anything:

1. **Events.** `sceneEvents` reads the timeline, the contacts the bake recorded and the bursts of the emitters, and the motion series of the shot. Every key of the timeline is an event (two when it has a start and an arrival that both matter), every contact and every burst is one. An event falls on a frame and says what is seen of it: its size, its peak speed and its place on screen, and whether it is seen at all, by the rules of the motion review. A move also says how it goes: its fastest moment, its two ends, the length of its way and the way itself, frame by frame.
2. **Plan.** `planSound` holds the events against a bank of sounds. One event makes one sound: the scene's own word first, then the voice of the node, then the bank's table of kinds. What is not seen is dropped, then what is too small, too slow or too weak, and then the density rules thin the rest: events of one kind close together sound as one, sounds of one kind keep a gap and a crowd of them is a few, any two starts keep a gap, a window of time holds only so many starts, and only so many voices sound at once. Where a rule has to choose, the event that matters more stays. A sound of the bank may set rules for its own events; it is then thinned by itself, and when it stands apart from the limits of the whole track its runs are showers: a pour heard impact by impact, beside the accents and not in their place. A sound may be fitted to a move: placed with its loudest point on the fastest moment of the move or on its end, panned along the way of the node, and played from the take that lasts most nearly as long as the move. Every dropped event is kept with its rule, for the report.
3. **Mix.** `mixVoices` brings each take to 48 kHz and to its pitch in one band-limited resampling, lays it on the first sample of its frame (or its loudest point on the first sample of the frame it is placed on), panned by its place on screen or, sample by sample, along the way of its node, and cuts it where the shot begins or ends. The track is written as a 24-bit PCM WAV exactly as long as the shot.

An author who cannot hear the track checks it by eye. The report names the node of every sound, and `renderSoundSheet` draws the sound sheet: a tile for every sound (one for a shower) with the frame at which it starts, the node it comes from ringed, and the sound, the kind of event and the time written on it. The sheet is the only picture `audio` draws, and it draws it with `render`.

A film is never crowded with sound, so the defaults of the rules are quiet, and they are data of the bank, not code. The engine holds no sound files. Sound is no part of the scene hash: it changes neither the bake nor a pixel. Guide: [SCENES.md](SCENES.md#sound).

## The bake cache

A cache file is `cache/<shot id>-<key>.bake` under the caller's working directory. The key is derived from:

- the **engine version**: the package version plus a fingerprint of the engine's sources, so any engine change invalidates old caches;
- the **physics version**: the exact pin of the rigid-body library;
- the **scene hash**: everything in the shot except the string tables (a translation does not re-bake), the intended exceptions of the reviews, and everything about sound (a sound key never re-bakes);
- the **seed**.

The file holds a JSON header (the key, slot names, contact events) and five binary sections: body poses, spring positions, the particle index, the particles and the particle velocities, as 32-bit floats. The evaluator reads state only from this representation, whether the bake was just computed or loaded from disk. Nothing is drawn from the velocities; the motion review reads them to know how fast a particle travels.

## Determinism

- Nothing that affects pixels uses `Math.random`, the clock, or the iteration order of an unordered container; a test scans the sources for the first two.
- Random numbers come from named streams of the shot's seed. A stream depends on its name only, not on how much other streams were used.
- Springs and particles advance at 1/240 s inside the bake; the camera (on a heading or on a path), intents, paths, levels, idle wander, blinks and pulses are closed-form functions of time.
- Rapier runs in its deterministic build; bodies join the world in a fixed order.
- Caches inside a process (paths, lit parts, text outlines, layouts) are keyed by their full input, so a hit returns what a miss would compute.
- GPU frames are exported with 4× MSAA. Stacked fills that share an edge (a crescent on its shape) resolve cleanly this way. On one machine and driver the GPU output is bit-exact between processes, between single frames and full renders, and between worker counts; the tests and the numbers in the README cover this. The CPU path is also deterministic but its pixels differ from the GPU's, and different GPUs or drivers are not expected to match bit for bit.

- The sound track of a shot is a pure function of the scene, the bake, the bank and the seed. Events fall on frames, the jitter of a sound comes from a stream of the shot's seed named by its event, and resampling and mixing are plain arithmetic in a fixed order: the same inputs give the same bytes.

## Output formats

- Preview: 1920×1080 at 60 fps, H.264 (x264 by default, NVENC with `--encoder nvenc`), BT.709.
- Any 16:9 width with `--width`; scenes are written in stage units, not pixels. A 4K master preset is planned.
