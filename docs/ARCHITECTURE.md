# Architecture

Phase 0 built a thin, real version of every module on the path from a scene to a video. This document says what each module does today and what is still planned. Anything not listed under "built" does not exist yet.

```
script + narration (word timings)                                   [planned, phase 4]
        │
        ▼
scene (TypeScript) ──► look ──► motion ──► physics bake ──► camera ──► render ──► ffmpeg
   │                    │         │             │                         │
   └── string tables    palette   intents,      rigid bodies, springs,    Skia GPU: layers, depth blur,
       per language     + light   springs       particles; cache keyed    glow, atmosphere, vignette
                                                by versions
        ▼
checks (before render): types, the rules a palette declares, string tables, glyphs, text fit per language
review (no render needed): the motion of every node as findings, ghost trails, speed graphs;
                           the soundness of the simulation as findings, close-ups
sound (no render needed):  the events of a shot; its sound track from a bank of sounds, in measure
```

## Data flow

1. **Describe.** A scene module builds a shot with the factory functions. The result is plain data: no functions, no classes. A `drive` is given a function; the factory calls it once for every frame of its stretch, there and then, and keeps only the numbers. `shot()` validates it as a whole.
2. **Compile.** The node trees are flattened in draw order, references and colours are resolved, the timeline is turned into per-node tracks, and every body, spring and emitter gets a slot in the bake. The scene hash is computed here, over everything except the string tables.
3. **Bake.** One process simulates everything with history at the fixed step and stores it per frame: rigid bodies (and their contact events), then springs, then particles. The pose stored for frame *n* is the state at *n*/60 s.
4. **Evaluate.** `evaluate(shot, bake, frame, language)` returns a display list: world transforms, link curves, pulse positions, label layouts, particle slices, opacities, the camera and the view of each layer. A node that is not fully opaque becomes a group holding its subtree; a node clipped to a shape, and a node that is being squashed, are wrapped in the same way. A soft node is such a group as well, and carries the width of its blur. A node with a height is evaluated at its top, so that what is in it and what reads its place follow, and its item carries the map from the top to the foot, from which the side, the roof and the openings are drawn. A group that casts shadows gets an item with the outlines of the shadows of its bodies on the ground; a group that sorts its children has them ordered far to near at each frame, a wall among them as one item to each stretch; a group whose shadows lie over its flat members lists those first and its shadows before the first raised one; in a group with a level of detail each body carries its size on screen and whether it is drawn plain; in a group with `tall` a body higher than the camera may reach carries a lift, the place and the foot through which it is drawn whole. A node on a path with heights is placed that high: over a plane its place is scaled about the point under the camera, as the top of a body is. In a layer that orders what it draws (a perspective layer, or a flat one with a depth on a listed node) the listed nodes are sorted at each frame, the farthest first; a group that lies in a plane of a perspective layer is wrapped with the map of that plane. A shot without either is evaluated as before, node for node. A child of a globe is placed at its longitude and latitude in the view of that frame, turned to its heading and faded behind the disc; a line on a globe carries the parts of it that face the viewer, as far as it is drawn, in the globe's own space. A shape with a haze is no item: its layer lists it apart, with the number of its items drawn before it, and the item of an emitter of streaks carries their tails. It has no state.
5. **Draw.** The renderer composites the display list on Skia and reads back raw RGBA. A group is drawn into a picture of its own and composited at its opacity. The lines of a label or a text with a second ink are filled twice from the same outline, its contours parted between the two inks, so every glyph stands where it stands in one ink. What lies in a plane of a perspective layer is drawn through a context of that plane (`src/render/plane.ts`): a current path that reaches behind the camera is drawn as a path given whole, which Skia cuts where the plane passes the camera, as it cuts every such path; a path wholly in front is drawn by the same call as ever. The pieces of a body with a roof, openings or a level of detail are traced straight onto the current path of the canvas, which costs no path object, and are given whole only when one of their points lies behind the camera. What feeds the halo of a layer is drawn in the same order as the layer itself, so a cover can erase the glow drawn before it. A layer with a heat haze is drawn in parts, each with its own halo: the parts before the haze are composited, the haze moves the frame as it is so far inside its outline (`src/render/haze.ts`: read back, moved on the CPU, written back, so the same numbers give the same pixels), and the parts after it are drawn over it. A layer without one is drawn in one part, as before. A node clipped to a shape with `clipMask` is drawn into a picture of its own and kept by a mask of the outline, because Skia's clip, an operation on paths, may fail on a long outline and drop the clip. A plane with `fog` is drawn into a picture of its own, and the haze is laid over it with one linear gradient: 1/w of the plane is linear across the canvas, so a gradient along the direction it changes in, its stops placed at the distances they stand for, gives every pixel its distance without a pixel worked out by hand. A `relief` is worked out pixel by pixel on the CPU (`src/render/relief.ts`): each pixel it covers is taken back into the field through the inverse of the transform it is drawn with, and its shade and height are read off the grid by bicubic interpolation; the shading of the grid is computed once and kept. A body taller than the camera is high (`tall`) is drawn through a lifted context (`liftedContext` in `src/render/plane.ts`) that projects every point of a path itself and gives Skia the projected outline: its points above the camera's height lie where the plane's w is negative, which Skia would cut. A shape that `stands` is drawn through a transform of its own whose last row carries its height, so its points rise from the plane as the camera sees them. Atmospheric perspective is applied to colours, not to pixels: every colour a far layer draws with is transformed before it is used.
6. **Encode.** Workers reserve RAM and VRAM for a scene, its bake and its encoder before loading a chunk. The group limit applies to concurrent drawing workers across commands. Chunk boundaries are independent of admission. The chunks are concatenated without re-encoding. A worker draws a frame while the one before is read back, but frees the canvases of that one before the next read-back starts. On the GPU a frame read back while the canvases of another are alive can come out with other pixels. Workers report progress to the parent. If one fails, the parent stops the others, removes the partial files and reports that worker's frame range.

Because frames are pure, a still, a motion strip or a contact sheet never needs a rendered video. With `alone`, each of their frames is the frame of a still of it alone, whatever was drawn before it (see Determinism).

## The motion review

A reviewer that cannot watch a video still has to judge motion. The review answers from the scene data, in three steps:

1. **Sample.** `sampleMotion` evaluates the shot at every frame without drawing: world transforms, opacities, the camera and the view of every layer. It keeps, for every placed node, its position, rotation, scale and opacity in the space of its layer, and its own motion in the space of its parent; for links, emitters and springs what their checks need. A node that nothing animates is sampled once.
2. **Detect.** Detectors read the series and return findings: plain data with a kind, a subject, a frame range, the measured value, the threshold and a usual fix. Every threshold is in `src/review/thresholds.ts`. Findings that the scene marks with `intended()` are taken out and listed as honoured.
3. **Show.** The report is printed as capped text and written as complete JSON. A ghost trail draws chosen nodes at every k-th frame over a dimmed frame; a speed graph plots speed and whatever else changes over time, with the findings marked.

The same scene always gives the same report and the same pictures. Guide: [MOTION-REVIEW.md](MOTION-REVIEW.md).

## The physics review

Whether the simulation is sound is answered from the bake, in one pass over the frames:

1. **Place.** Every rigid body gets the collider the world gave it: a convex core (a point, a segment, a polygon) and a radius around it, at the baked pose of the frame. The review builds this geometry itself and does not ask the physics library; a test holds the two against each other.
2. **Pair.** Bodies whose bounding circles are near are found by a sweep along x; for each pair the distance, or the depth, of the two colliders is worked out. From it come the overlaps, the piles (bodies that touch) and what a body can have touched within a frame.
3. **Detect.** Detectors read the poses and the pairs: an overlap that lasts, a centre that crosses a fixed body or a gap, a body below everything, swings of a body at rest, a pile that travels at the end, speed that nothing in touch can have given, a path bent by a body that is not drawn, numbers that are not numbers. Every threshold is in `src/review/physics-thresholds.ts`. What is not seen is not reported.
4. **Show.** The report has the form of the motion report. A close-up draws a few frames of the place, enlarged, with the bodies ringed.

It is synchronous and takes a fraction of a second on a shot with five hundred bodies, so it runs in every `skink3 check`. When the physics of the engine changes, this review changes with it in the same piece of work. Guide: [PHYSICS-REVIEW.md](PHYSICS-REVIEW.md).

## The sound of a shot

The sound effects of a shot are made from what happens in it, in three steps, none of which draws anything:

1. **Events.** `sceneEvents` reads the timeline, the contacts the bake recorded and the bursts of the emitters, and the motion series of the shot. Every key of the timeline is an event (two when it has a start and an arrival that both matter), every contact and every burst is one. An event falls on a frame and says what is seen of it: its size, its peak speed and its place on screen, and whether it is seen at all, by the rules of the motion review. A move also says how it goes: its fastest moment, its two ends, the length of its way and the way itself, frame by frame.
2. **Plan.** `planSound` holds the events against a bank of sounds. One event makes one sound: the scene's own word first, then the voice of the node, then the bank's table of kinds. What is not seen is dropped, then what is too small, too slow or too weak, and then the density rules thin the rest: events of one kind close together sound as one, sounds of one kind keep a gap and a crowd of them is a few, any two starts keep a gap, a window of time holds only so many starts, and only so many voices sound at once. Where a rule has to choose, the event that matters more stays. A sound of the bank may set rules for its own events; it is then thinned by itself, and when it stands apart from the limits of the whole track its runs are showers: a pour heard impact by impact, beside the accents and not in their place. A sound may be fitted to a move: placed with its loudest point on the fastest moment of the move or on its end, panned along the way of the node, and played from the take that lasts most nearly as long as the move. Every dropped event is kept with its rule, for the report.
3. **Mix.** `mixVoices` brings each take to 48 kHz and to its pitch in one band-limited resampling, lays it on the first sample of its frame (or its loudest point on the first sample of the frame it is placed on), panned by its place on screen or, sample by sample, along the way of its node, and cuts it where the shot begins or ends. The track is written as a 24-bit PCM WAV exactly as long as the shot.

An author who cannot hear the track checks it by eye. The report names the node of every sound, and `renderSoundSheet` draws the sound sheet: a tile for every sound (one for a shower) with the frame at which it starts, the node it comes from ringed, and the sound, the kind of event and the time written on it. The sheet is the only picture `audio` draws, and it draws it with `render`.

A film is never crowded with sound, so the defaults of the rules are quiet, and they are data of the bank, not code. The engine holds no sound files. Sound is no part of the scene hash: it changes neither the bake nor a pixel. Guide: [SCENES.md](SCENES.md#sound).

## The bake cache

A cache file is `cache/<shot id>-<key>.bake` under the caller's working directory. The key is derived from:

- the **engine version**: the package version plus a fingerprint of the engine's sources, so any engine change invalidates old caches;
- the **physics version**: the exact pin of the rigid-body library;
- the **scene hash**: everything in the shot except the string tables (a translation does not re-bake), the intended exceptions of the reviews, and everything about sound (a sound key never re-bakes);
- the **seed**.

The file holds a JSON header (the key, slot names, contact events) and five binary sections: body poses, spring positions, the particle index, the particles and the particle velocities, as 32-bit floats. A shot with an emitter of streaks has a sixth, the tails of the particles (where each streak ends, from the particle); a shot without one names no tails, and its file is what it was. The evaluator reads state only from this representation, whether the bake was just computed or loaded from disk. Nothing is drawn from the velocities; the motion review reads them to know how fast a particle travels.

## Determinism

- Nothing that affects pixels uses `Math.random`, the clock, or the iteration order of an unordered container; a test scans the sources for the first two.
- Random numbers come from named streams of the shot's seed. A stream depends on its name only, not on how much other streams were used.
- Springs and particles advance at 1/240 s inside the bake; the camera (on a heading or on a path, with its keys and its zoom keys), intents, paths, levels, idle wander, blinks and pulses are closed-form functions of time; so are the view of a perspective layer and the order it draws in. A drive is a table of numbers in the scene, part of the scene hash, read back in a fixed way: the function behind it is never called again.
- Rapier runs in its deterministic build; bodies join the world in a fixed order.
- Text is drawn with the bundled fonts only. The canvas library would draw a character that none of them has with a font of the machine; the glyph check fails on such a character before a render starts.
- Caches inside a process (paths, lit parts, text outlines, layouts) are keyed by their full input, so a hit returns what a miss would compute.
- The path operations of the drawing library are not exact: at some offsets the difference of a path and its shifted copy is not the crescent. A lit part is checked and repaired as a pure function of the shape and the light, so a frame is the same frame however often it is drawn; where the library's result is sound, it is used as it is.
- GPU frames are exported with 4× MSAA. Stacked fills that share an edge (a crescent on its shape) resolve cleanly this way. On one machine and driver the GPU output is bit-exact between processes and between runs that draw the same frames in the same order, at different queue loads; the tests and the numbers in the README cover this. The drawing library keeps the triangles of a curved outline for about a second, by its path object and, for a small path, by its points, and draws the outline again with them, also at another scale; the caches of the engine keep their path objects from frame to frame. So a frame drawn soon after frames of another zoom, in a chunk of a render or in a still of several frames, may differ from the frame alone by a few pixels of such an edge, and a frame of a render may move by as much with where its chunk starts, that is with the worker count and the chunk length. A still of one frame, the first frame of every chunk, and every frame drawn with `alone` are the frame alone: before such a frame the process waits until the GPU has rested from the frames before (`GPU_FORGETS_MS`); the library has no call that drops what it keeps sooner. A render draws its frames in a row, as before, for its speed and its pixels. Each chunk starts with a key frame of the encoded file. The CPU path is also deterministic but its pixels differ from the GPU's, and different GPUs or drivers are not expected to match bit for bit.

- The sound track of a shot is a pure function of the scene, the bake, the bank and the seed. Events fall on frames, the jitter of a sound comes from a stream of the shot's seed named by its event, and resampling and mixing are plain arithmetic in a fixed order: the same inputs give the same bytes.

## Output formats

- Preview: 1920×1080 at 60 fps, H.264 (x264 by default, NVENC with `--encoder nvenc`), BT.709.
- Any 16:9 width with `--width`; scenes are written in stage units, not pixels. A 4K master preset is planned.
